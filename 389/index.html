Course Description

This course teaches fundamental skills of digital game development alongside the basic principles of level design. Over the semester, students will learn concepts related to the implementation and presentation of digital games using computer graphics, then demonstrate that learning through practical projects focused on specific game-development techniques.

Through these projects, students will practice level-design preproduction, layout, blockout, and production.

The course also covers professional communication for game designers, including techniques that support collaboration and conflict resolution.

Grading — Conscientiousness

Your grade in this course is based on conscientiousness: attending class and having work ready when it is scheduled for playtest.

Attendance and Punctuality

You receive one forgiven absence over the semester. Beginning with the second absence, each additional absence lowers the final course grade by 3 points. For example, a final grade of 100 becomes 97.

Four late arrivals, accumulated across the semester, count as one absence. That absence is included in the same forgiveness and subsequent deductions.

If Peter is ever late to class, attendance will not be taken that day. In addition, every student receives one additional forgiven absence to use later in the semester.

Playtest Builds

You receive one forgiven playtest without a working build over the semester. After that, each scheduled playtest for which you do not provide a working build ready for in-class play lowers the final course grade by 5 points.

Your grade begins at 100. Attendance and playtest-build deductions accumulate: for example, a second absence lowers a 100 to 97, and a second playtest without a working build lowers a 100 to 95. A final grade of 90–100 is an A; 80–89.9 is a B; and so on.

Disruptive Behavior

Behavior that persistently or grossly interferes with classroom activities is considered disruptive behavior and may be subject to disciplinary action. A student responsible for disruptive behavior may be required to leave class pending discussion and resolution of the problem and may be reported to the Office of Student Judicial Affairs for disciplinary action.
